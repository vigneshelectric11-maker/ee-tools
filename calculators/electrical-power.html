Electrical Power Formula

P = V × I

For a DC circuit, power equals voltage multiplied by current.

Electrical Power Formulas

The power equation depends on whether the electrical system is DC, single-phase AC, or balanced three-phase AC.

System Real Power Formula Apparent Power Formula
DC P = V × I Not separated into VA/PF components
Single-Phase AC P = V × I × PF S = V × I
Three-Phase AC P = √3 × VLL × I × PF S = √3 × VLL × I

Real, Apparent, and Reactive Power

In AC systems, real power is the portion of electrical power associated with useful energy transfer and is measured in watts or kilowatts. Apparent power is the product of RMS voltage and current, with the appropriate phase-system factor, and is measured in volt-amperes or kilovolt-amperes.

For the sinusoidal power-factor model used by this calculator, reactive power is calculated from the relationship between apparent and real power:

Q = √(S² − P²)

The phase angle is calculated from: φ = cos⁻¹(PF).

Example Calculations

Single-phase example: A 240 V load drawing 10 A at a power factor of 0.80 has 1.920 kW of real power, 2.400 kVA of apparent power, and 1.440 kVAR of reactive power.

Three-phase example: A balanced 480 V three-phase load drawing 125 A at a power factor of 0.92 has approximately 95.609 kW of real power and 103.923 kVA of apparent power.

Engineering Note

This calculator is intended for educational and preliminary engineering calculations. AC results assume sinusoidal steady-state quantities, and the three-phase calculation assumes a balanced system using line-to-line voltage and line current. Actual electrical systems may require additional consideration of harmonics, unbalance, equipment characteristics, applicable codes, and manufacturer data.

Always verify design, installation, protection, conductor sizing, and equipment requirements using applicable standards and qualified engineering judgment.
