Transformer Current Formulas

Single Phase: I = (kVA × 1000) ÷ V
Three Phase: I = (kVA × 1000) ÷ (√3 × V)
I is rated full-load current in amperes, kVA is the transformer apparent-power rating, and V is the rated winding voltage. For three-phase transformers, V is the rated line-to-line voltage.

Understanding transformer current

A transformer transfers electrical power between its primary and secondary windings while changing voltage and current levels.

For approximately the same apparent power, a lower-voltage winding carries more current than a higher-voltage winding.

Rated full-load current can be calculated from transformer kVA and rated winding voltage.

Transformer current formulas

Transformer Type Formula Current Unit
Single Phase I = (kVA × 1000) ÷ V A
Three Phase I = (kVA × 1000) ÷ (√3 × V) A
Voltage Ratio Ratio = Vprimary ÷ Vsecondary Ratio

Example calculation

Consider a 75 kVA three-phase transformer with a 480 V primary and 208 V secondary.

Primary current: 75,000 ÷ (√3 × 480) ≈ 90.211 A .

Secondary current: 75,000 ÷ (√3 × 208) ≈ 208.179 A .

The voltage ratio is approximately 2.308 : 1, so this is a step-down transformer.

Engineering considerations

These calculations use the transformer nameplate kVA rating and rated voltages to determine nominal full-load current.

Actual equipment and conductor design may also require consideration of transformer impedance, inrush current, temperature rise, efficiency, conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding, harmonics, installation conditions, and applicable electrical codes.

Transformer nameplate information and manufacturer documentation should be used when available.
