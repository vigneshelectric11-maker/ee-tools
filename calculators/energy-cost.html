Energy Cost Formulas

Energy (kWh) = Power (kW) × Time (hours)
Cost = Energy (kWh) × Electricity Rate
Monthly Energy = Daily Energy × Operating Days
Electrical energy is commonly measured in kilowatt-hours. A 1 kW load operating for one hour consumes 1 kWh.

Understanding electricity cost

Electrical equipment consumes energy according to its average electrical power and operating time.

For example, a 1 kW load operating for one hour consumes 1 kWh of electrical energy.

Utility bills may also include service charges, taxes, demand charges, fuel adjustments, time-of-use rates, and other fees. This calculator estimates the energy-charge portion using the electricity rate entered.

Example calculation

Consider one 1,500 W electrical load operating for 8 hours per day, 30 days per month, with an electricity rate of $0.18/kWh.

Convert power to kilowatts: 1500 W ÷ 1000 = 1.5 kW .

Daily energy use: 1.5 × 8 = 12 kWh .

Monthly energy use: 12 × 30 = 360 kWh .

Estimated monthly energy cost: 360 × $0.18 = $64.80 .

If the same monthly usage pattern continues for 12 months, the estimated yearly energy use is 4,320 kWh and the estimated yearly energy cost is $777.60.

Engineering considerations

Actual energy consumption may differ from equipment nameplate power when a device cycles on and off, operates at partial load, or changes operating mode.

Motors, HVAC equipment, variable-frequency drives, compressors, heaters, pumps, and other loads can have operating profiles that vary substantially throughout the day.

For more accurate estimates, use measured average power or actual energy-meter data when available.

Important cost estimate note

The daily cost shown represents the cost for one operating day at the entered number of hours. Monthly cost multiplies that amount by the entered operating days per month.

The yearly estimate assumes the same monthly energy-use pattern continues for 12 months. It does not automatically account for seasonal usage or changing utility rates.
