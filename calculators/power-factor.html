Power Factor Correction Formula

Qc = P × [tan(φ₁) − tan(φ₂)]
Qc is the required capacitive reactive power, P is real power, φ₁ is the existing power-factor angle, and φ₂ is the target power-factor angle.

Power factor correction formulas

For a lagging load, capacitor compensation reduces the reactive power that must be supplied by the upstream electrical system.

Quantity Formula Unit
Apparent Power S = P ÷ PF kVA
Current Reactive Power Q₁ = P × tan(cos⁻¹(PF₁)) kVAR
Target Reactive Power Q₂ = P × tan(cos⁻¹(PF₂)) kVAR
Capacitor Size Qc = Q₁ − Q₂ kVAR
Phase Angle φ = cos⁻¹(PF) Degrees

Example calculation

Assume a load consumes 100 kW at a lagging power factor of 0.80 and the desired target power factor is 0.95.

Current apparent power: 100 ÷ 0.80 = 125.000 kVA.

Current reactive power: 75.000 kVAR.

Target reactive power: approximately 32.868 kVAR.

Required capacitor size: approximately 42.132 kVAR.

Why power factor correction matters

Improving power factor reduces apparent power and, for a given system voltage and real power, can reduce the line current required to deliver that real power.

Lower current can reduce losses and may free capacity in conductors, transformers, switchgear, and other distribution equipment.

Practical capacitor-bank selection should also consider system voltage, frequency, harmonics, resonance, load variation, switching requirements, equipment ratings, and manufacturer recommendations.

Important engineering note

This calculator assumes an inductive or lagging load that is being corrected using capacitive reactive power. It does not model an already-leading load or automatically determine harmonic-filter or detuned-reactor requirements.

The calculated kVAR is an engineering estimate. Actual capacitor-bank selection normally uses commercially available ratings and should be verified against the specific electrical system before installation.
