Three-Phase Power Formula

P = √3 × V × I × PF
Where P is real power, V is line-to-line RMS voltage, I is line current, and PF is power factor. The formula assumes a balanced three-phase system.

Three-phase power formulas

In a balanced three-phase system, electrical power can be expressed as real power, apparent power, and reactive power.

Quantity Formula Unit
Real Power P = √3 × V × I × PF kW
Apparent Power S = √3 × V × I kVA
Reactive Power Q = √(S² − P²) kVAR

How to use this calculator

Enter the three-phase line-to-line voltage, line current, and power factor. For example, a 480 V three-phase system operating at 125 A with a power factor of 0.92 produces approximately 95.609 kW of real power, 103.923 kVA of apparent power, and 40.729 kVAR of reactive power.

The calculator assumes a balanced three-phase system and uses RMS quantities. Always verify actual system conditions, equipment ratings, applicable electrical codes, and engineering requirements before using calculated values for design or field decisions.

What do kW, kVA, and kVAR mean?

Real power, measured in kilowatts (kW), represents the power that performs useful work. Apparent power, measured in kilovolt-amperes (kVA), represents the total RMS voltage-current product supplied to the load. Reactive power, measured in kVAR, represents the non-working component associated with reactive energy exchange in AC systems.

Power factor is the ratio of real power to apparent power. A power factor closer to 1 means that a greater portion of the apparent power is being converted into real power.
