Single-Phase AC Power

P = V × I × PF
Where P is real power, V is RMS voltage, I is RMS current, and PF is power factor.

Single-phase power formulas

For a single-phase AC circuit, apparent power is the product of RMS voltage and RMS current. Real power additionally depends on power factor.

Quantity Formula Unit
Real Power P = V × I × PF kW
Apparent Power S = V × I kVA
Reactive Power Q = √(S² − P²) kVAR
Power Factor PF = P ÷ S Unitless
Phase Angle φ = cos⁻¹(PF) Degrees

How to use this calculator

Enter the RMS voltage, RMS current, and power factor of the single-phase load. The calculator will determine real power, apparent power, reactive power, and phase angle.

For example, a 240 V load drawing 10 A at a power factor of 0.80 has 2.400 kVA of apparent power, 1.920 kW of real power, 1.440 kVAR of reactive power, and a phase angle of approximately 36.870°.

This calculator assumes a sinusoidal single-phase AC system in steady state. For nonlinear loads, harmonics and true power measurements may require additional analysis.

Power factor and phase angle

Power factor represents the ratio of real power to apparent power. For a sinusoidal system, the magnitude of the phase angle can be calculated from φ = cos⁻¹(PF).

This calculator reports the magnitude of reactive power and phase angle. It does not distinguish between leading and lagging power factor because the input is a positive power-factor magnitude.
