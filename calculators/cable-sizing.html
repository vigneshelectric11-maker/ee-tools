Cable Sizing Method

Single Phase VD = (2 × K × I × L) ÷ CM
Three Phase VD = (√3 × K × I × L) ÷ CM
VD% = (VD ÷ V) × 100
K = 12.9 for copper and 21.2 for aluminum in this simplified voltage-drop model. The selected conductor must also meet the calculator's built-in ampacity screening value.

How this cable sizing calculator works

Conductor sizing involves more than selecting a wire that can carry the expected current. Long circuit runs may require a larger conductor because of voltage drop.

This calculator checks the available conductor sizes from smallest to largest. Each conductor must pass both the built-in ampacity screen and the selected voltage-drop limit before it can be returned.

The result should be treated as a preliminary engineering estimate rather than a final conductor specification.

Example calculation

Consider a 240 V single-phase circuit carrying 40 A through a 100 ft one-way copper conductor run, with a maximum desired voltage drop of 3%.

A 10 AWG conductor does not pass this calculator's built-in 40 A ampacity screen.

The next size, 8 AWG copper, has a built-in screening ampacity of 50 A.

Estimated voltage drop: approximately 6.251 V .

Estimated voltage-drop percentage: approximately 2.605% .

Because 8 AWG passes both screening conditions, the calculator returns 8 AWG.

Important ampacity limitation

The ampacity values used by this calculator are a simplified screening table only. They do not represent every conductor type, insulation system, temperature rating, terminal limitation, installation method, or electrical-code condition.

The design current entered should already reflect any load calculations or sizing factors that apply to the installation. This calculator does not automatically apply continuous-load multipliers, motor-specific sizing requirements, or other code-based adjustments.

Engineering considerations

Final conductor sizing may require consideration of conductor insulation rating, terminal temperature rating, ambient temperature, number of current-carrying conductors, raceway or cable installation, continuous loads, motors, overcurrent protection, grounding, equipment requirements, and other applicable rules.

For AC systems, actual voltage drop can also be affected by conductor resistance at operating temperature, conductor reactance, circuit power factor, conductor arrangement, and parallel conductors.

Always verify the final conductor selection using the applicable electrical code, project requirements, equipment documentation, and site-specific conditions.
