Power Triangle

kVA² = kW² + kVAR²
Real power, reactive power, and apparent power form the electrical power triangle. Power factor is the ratio of real power to apparent power.

kW, kVA, kVAR and power factor

Real power represents the power doing useful work. Apparent power represents the combined magnitude of real and reactive power. Reactive power represents the magnitude of the reactive component of AC power.

Power factor expresses the ratio between real power and apparent power.

Quantity Formula Unit
Real Power kW = kVA × PF kW
Apparent Power kVA = kW ÷ PF kVA
Reactive Power kVAR = √(kVA² − kW²) kVAR
Power Factor PF = kW ÷ kVA Unitless
Phase Angle φ = cos⁻¹(PF) Degrees

Example calculation

Suppose a system has 100 kW of real power and 125 kVA of apparent power.

The power factor is PF = 100 ÷ 125 = 0.800.

The reactive power is kVAR = √(125² − 100²) = 75 kVAR.

The corresponding phase-angle magnitude is approximately 36.870°.

Why power factor matters

A lower power factor means more apparent power is required to deliver the same amount of real power. This can increase current and the required capacity of electrical conductors and equipment.

In practical installations, power factor can be affected by inductive loads such as motors and transformers, as well as nonlinear loads and harmonics.

Important calculation note

This calculator treats kVAR and phase angle as magnitudes. It does not distinguish between leading and lagging reactive power because all entered quantities are treated as positive magnitudes.
