Voltage Drop Formulas

Single Phase: VD = (2 × K × I × L) ÷ CM
Three Phase: VD = (√3 × K × I × L) ÷ CM
VD is voltage drop in volts, K is the conductor resistivity constant, I is current in amperes, L is one-way conductor length in feet, and CM is conductor circular-mil area.

Understanding voltage drop

Voltage drop is the reduction in voltage that occurs as electrical current flows through a conductor.

Longer conductor runs, higher current, and smaller conductor sizes generally produce greater voltage drop.

Increasing conductor size reduces conductor resistance and can therefore reduce voltage drop.

Example calculation

Consider a 240 V single-phase circuit carrying 40 A through 100 ft of 6 AWG copper conductor.

For 6 AWG copper, CM = 26,240.

The calculated voltage drop is approximately 3.933 V.

Voltage drop percentage is approximately 1.639%.

Estimated voltage available at the load is 236.067 V.

Voltage drop design guidance

Designers often aim for relatively low voltage drop to support equipment performance and efficient operation. Values around 3% for a branch circuit and around 5% for combined feeder and branch-circuit voltage drop are commonly referenced design targets, but the applicable requirements depend on the specific installation, equipment, jurisdiction, and governing standard.

The assessment shown by this calculator is therefore general design guidance rather than an automatic determination of code compliance.

Engineering considerations

This calculator uses the simplified K-factor method for estimating conductor voltage drop.

Actual AC voltage drop can also be influenced by conductor temperature, power factor, conductor reactance, installation configuration, conductor spacing, and other electrical system conditions.

Conductor ampacity is not checked by this voltage-drop calculator. Always verify conductor sizing, ampacity, terminal limitations, protection requirements, voltage drop, and applicable electrical codes separately.
