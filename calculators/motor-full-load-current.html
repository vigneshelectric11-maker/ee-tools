Motor Current Formulas

Single Phase: I = Pout ÷ (V × PF × η)
Three Phase: I = Pout ÷ (√3 × V × PF × η)
Horsepower Conversion: Pout = HP × 746
I is estimated motor line current in amperes, Pout is mechanical output power in watts, V is rated line voltage, PF is power factor, and η is efficiency expressed as a decimal.

Understanding motor full-load current

This calculator estimates the electrical current required for a motor to deliver its rated mechanical output power, using the entered efficiency and power factor.

Motor current depends on mechanical load, supply voltage, efficiency, power factor, motor design, and operating conditions.

For three-phase motors, the calculation uses the √3 relationship between line-to-line voltage, line current, and apparent power.

Motor current formulas

Calculation Formula Unit
Horsepower to Watts Pout = HP × 746 W
Electrical Input Power Pin = Pout ÷ η kW
Apparent Power S = Pin ÷ PF kVA
Single-Phase Current I = Pout ÷ (V × PF × η) A
Three-Phase Current I = Pout ÷ (√3 × V × PF × η) A

Example calculation

Consider a 25 HP three-phase motor operating at 460 V with a power factor of 0.85 and efficiency of 90%.

Mechanical output power: 25 × 746 = 18,650 W = 18.650 kW .

Electrical input power: 18.650 ÷ 0.90 ≈ 20.722 kW .

Apparent power: 20.722 ÷ 0.85 ≈ 24.379 kVA .

Estimated motor current: 18,650 ÷ (√3 × 460 × 0.85 × 0.90) ≈ 30.608 A .

Important engineering note

This is an engineering estimate calculated from motor output power, efficiency, power factor, voltage, and phase. It is not a substitute for the actual motor nameplate current.

It should also not be confused with standardized motor full-load-current values that may be specified by electrical codes for conductor, overload, short-circuit, ground-fault, or branch-circuit calculations.

For equipment design and code compliance, use the applicable electrical code, motor nameplate data, manufacturer information, and the specific requirements for the installation.

Starting current and motor load

This calculator estimates steady-state current near rated mechanical output. Motor starting or locked-rotor current can be several times higher and is not calculated here.

A lightly loaded motor may also operate at a different efficiency and power factor than the values at rated load.
