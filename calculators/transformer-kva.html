Transformer kVA Formula

kVA = V × I ÷ 1000

For a single-phase transformer, apparent power equals voltage multiplied by current, divided by 1,000.

Transformer kVA Formulas

Transformer apparent power is commonly expressed in kilovolt-amperes (kVA). The required formula depends on whether the system is single-phase or balanced three-phase.

System Formula
Single-Phase kVA = V × I ÷ 1000
Three-Phase kVA = √3 × VLL × I ÷ 1000

How to Calculate Transformer kVA

For a single-phase transformer, multiply the RMS voltage by the current and divide the result by 1,000.

For example, a single-phase transformer operating at 240 V and 100 A has an apparent power of:

kVA = 240 × 100 ÷ 1000 = 24 kVA

For a balanced three-phase transformer, multiply the line-to-line RMS voltage by the line current and by √3, then divide by 1,000.

Example Calculations

Single-phase example: A transformer rated at 240 V and 100 A has an apparent power of 24 kVA.

Three-phase example: A balanced three-phase transformer operating at 480 V and 100 A has an apparent power of approximately 83.138 kVA.

kVA vs kW

kVA represents apparent power, while kW represents real power. For AC loads, the relationship between the two includes power factor:

kW = kVA × Power Factor

Because transformers are commonly rated according to apparent power, their nameplate capacity is typically expressed in VA or kVA rather than kW.

Engineering Note

This calculator determines apparent power from voltage and current. Three-phase calculations assume a balanced system and use line-to-line RMS voltage and line current.

Calculated kVA should not automatically be treated as the required transformer nameplate rating. Transformer selection may also depend on load characteristics, continuous loading, starting or inrush current, harmonics, temperature, future expansion, applicable standards, and manufacturer data.
